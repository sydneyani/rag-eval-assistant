# Item 1. Business

## Business overview {business-overview}

Helios Systems, Inc. designs inference appliances and the software that schedules work across them. The company was founded in 2014 and is headquartered in Austin, Texas. Mira Ellison is the chief executive officer. Adrian Cho is the chief financial officer. The common stock uses the ticker HLS on this synthetic filing.

The fiscal year ends on the Sunday closest to January 31. Fiscal 2024 ended on February 2, 2025. Fiscal 2023 ended on January 28, 2024. References to a fiscal year in this report mean the year then ended, not the calendar year whose number matches the label.

Helios sells to cloud operators, research laboratories, and large enterprises that run their own clusters. The company does not offer a consumer product. Management describes the business as two reporting lines inside one segment: data-center hardware, and software and support. Hardware is dominated by the Helios Inference Rack. Software is dominated by the Helios Fabric Compiler, which includes the scheduler technology acquired with Northline Scheduler.

The company operates one flagship campus, Quarry Ridge, outside San Antonio, and smaller leased halls in Ashburn and Hillsboro. Manufacturing of rack sheet metal and final assembly is done by contract manufacturers. Accelerator modules and several custom silicon devices are purchased from suppliers, of which Lattice Silicon is the primary wafer source under a take-or-pay agreement described in the risk factors.

## Products {products}

The flagship product in fiscal 2024 was the third-generation Helios Inference Rack, which the company calls HIR-3. The fiscal 2024 average selling price of an HIR-3 rack was $428,000, and the company shipped 6,150 HIR-3 racks during fiscal 2024. HIR-3 rack revenue was $2.63 billion. The remaining data-center hardware revenue of $0.48 billion came from networking trays, spares, and service appliances that ship alongside the racks.

In fiscal 2023 the average selling price of an HIR-3 rack was $401,000 and the company shipped 5,020 racks. The increase in average selling price reflects a mix shift toward configurations with more accelerator memory, not a list-price increase on the base rack. Configurations with aggregate accelerator memory above 96 GB are the SKUs that require an export license for shipment to listed countries. Those licensing limits are discussed under export controls and are not repeated as a separate product policy.

The Helios Fabric Compiler is the software stack that places models, reserves accelerator memory, and retries failed workers. A one-year or longer Fabric Compiler subscription is what management means by a software attach. Attach rates for the fourth quarter are reported in the shareholder letter rather than in this Form 10-K, because the letter is where the company discusses quarterly mix. The compiler is delivered as a subscription. Support revenue is the portion of software and support that covers hardware service contracts, and it is not broken out from software in the consolidated totals.

Customers who buy an HIR-3 rack without a compiler subscription can still run open schedulers. Helios does not represent that third-party schedulers are unsupported, and it does not represent that they perform at the same utilization. Utilization figures in this report are measured only on company-operated clusters, not on racks sitting in a customer's own data hall.

## Customers and backlog {customers}

Helios had 1,840 customers at the end of fiscal 2024. Customer A accounted for 11 percent of fiscal 2024 revenue. No other customer accounted for more than 10 percent of fiscal 2024 revenue. Customer A is a cloud operator. The company does not name Customer A in this filing. The concentration risk, including the fiscal 2023 comparison, is discussed in the risk factors.

Backlog was $2.05 billion as of February 2, 2025, compared with $1.48 billion as of January 28, 2024. Backlog means unfulfilled orders that the company believes are firm, but a portion of backlog can be cancelled or delayed without a material penalty. Management does not treat backlog as a forecast of the next year's revenue. The increase came from multi-rack orders placed in the second half of the year, several of which requested delivery after the HIR-3 memory configurations cleared export review.

The company sells primarily through a direct sales force. Distributors accounted for less than 5 percent of fiscal 2024 revenue. Payment terms for Customer A are net 45 days. Payment terms for other customers are typically net 30 days. The company does not offer extended financing of racks, and it does not hold customer racks as leased assets.

# Item 7. Management's discussion and analysis

## Results of operations {results}

Fiscal 2024 revenue was $4.82 billion, compared with fiscal 2023 revenue of $4.08 billion, an increase of 18.1 percent. The increase was volume, not price alone. Rack shipments rose, and software and support grew faster than hardware because more of the installed base carried a compiler subscription into the renewal year.

Data-center hardware revenue was $3.11 billion in fiscal 2024, compared with $2.74 billion in fiscal 2023. Software and support revenue was $1.71 billion in fiscal 2024, compared with $1.34 billion in fiscal 2023. Hardware remains the larger line. Software is the line management cites when it explains the gross-margin expansion, because compiler subscriptions carry a higher margin than racks.

Revenue is recognized when control of a rack transfers, which is generally on delivery to the customer's carrier. Subscription revenue is recognized ratably over the subscription term. The company does not recognize revenue on cancelled backlog. Unsatisfied performance obligations related to subscriptions that have been billed but not yet earned are recorded as deferred revenue and are not included in the backlog figure above.

A single customer, Customer A, is large enough that its ordering pattern moves the quarterly total. The fourth-quarter shareholder letter is the document that discusses quarterly revenue. This annual discussion compares fiscal years only. Readers who want the fourth-quarter fiscal 2024 total should use the shareholder letter dated February 20, 2025, not a quarterly column in this Form 10-K. This report does not include a fourth-quarter revenue line.

## Margins and income {margins}

Gross margin was 61.4 percent in fiscal 2024, compared with 58.2 percent in fiscal 2023. The expansion came from software mix and from a lower cost of accelerator memory per rack as the Lattice Silicon agreement moved further down its volume schedule. Gross margin is consolidated. The company does not disclose a separate gross margin for hardware and for software.

Operating income was $986 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $641 million in fiscal 2023. Operating expenses grew more slowly than revenue. The largest operating expense outside of cost of revenue is research and development, which is discussed in its own section and is included in the operating-income figure.

Net income was $742 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $509 million in fiscal 2023. The effective tax rate was 17.8 percent in fiscal 2024 and 19.4 percent in fiscal 2023. The rate declined because a larger share of pretax income was earned in jurisdictions where the company holds research credits, and because discrete tax benefits related to stock compensation were higher in fiscal 2024. The company does not expect the fiscal 2024 rate to be a floor. A change in the geographic mix of income would move the rate back toward the fiscal 2023 level.

Interest expense on the term debt is included in the gap between operating income and pretax income. The company does not present adjusted net income, and it does not add stock-based compensation back in the totals above. All figures in this section are as reported under U.S. GAAP as used by this synthetic issuer.

## Research and development {research}

Research and development expense was $890 million in fiscal 2024, or 18.5 percent of revenue, compared with $736 million in fiscal 2023. The increase funded HIR-4 silicon bring-up, compiler work on longer context windows, and the team that joined with the Northline Scheduler acquisition. Research and development is expensed as incurred. The company capitalizes almost none of its internal software development cost, because the projects do not meet the threshold it uses for capitalization until a release is in customer sampling.

About 4,100 of the company's employees worked in research and development at year end. The headcount figure for the whole company is in the employees section. Research and development headcount is not a subset that should be added on top of the company total.

The company announced nothing about HIR-4 general availability in this Form 10-K. The sampling window and the general-availability month are stated in the shareholder letter. Using this section to answer a question about the HIR-4 date would be a mistake. This section records the expense, not the roadmap.

## Liquidity and capital resources {liquidity}

Cash and cash equivalents were $2.36 billion as of February 2, 2025, compared with $1.91 billion as of January 28, 2024. Total debt was $1.50 billion as of February 2, 2025, compared with $1.22 billion as of January 28, 2024. The debt is a single term loan with a remaining maturity in 2029. There is no convertible note. The company had no drawings on its revolving credit facility at year end. The facility size is $400 million and it was undrawn.

Capital expenditures were $1.14 billion in fiscal 2024 and $860 million in fiscal 2023. Most of the fiscal 2024 spend was power and cooling construction at Quarry Ridge, plus test equipment for HIR-4. Capital expenditures do not include the Lattice Silicon purchase commitment, which is an operating purchase and not a capital lease. The remaining purchase commitment is described in the supply risk factor.

The company believes the cash balance and the undrawn revolver are enough to fund the next twelve months of capital expenditure and the Lattice minimums, assuming Customer A pays on terms. That belief is a liquidity judgment, not a forecast of revenue. Free cash flow is not a metric the company presents, and this section does not compute one.

## Employees {employees}

Helios had 12,450 employees as of February 2, 2025, compared with 10,220 employees as of January 28, 2024. The increase is mostly engineering and deployment staff hired into the Austin headquarters and the Quarry Ridge campus. Approximately 4,100 employees worked in research and development. Approximately 1,200 employees worked in the deployment organization that brings racks into service at customer sites and at Quarry Ridge.

None of the employees are represented by a labor union. The company has not experienced a work stoppage. Attrition in fiscal 2024 was not disclosed as a number in this report. Compensation expense is included in the operating results and is not broken out here.

# Item 1A. Risk factors

## Export controls {risk-export}

Revenue from jurisdictions subject to enhanced export controls was 14 percent of fiscal 2024 revenue, compared with 11 percent of fiscal 2023 revenue. The increase is mix. A larger share of shipments in the year were high-memory racks, and several of the destinations for those racks are on the list of jurisdictions that require a license.

HIR-3 configurations with aggregate accelerator memory above 96 GB require a license for shipment to listed countries. The base HIR-3 configuration, which ships with 96 GB or less of aggregate accelerator memory, did not require that license in fiscal 2024. License timing is unpredictable. A delay does not cancel the order automatically, but it does move revenue from one quarter to another, and a denial would remove the order. The company cannot predict denial rates. It had no material denials in fiscal 2024. That history is not a representation about fiscal 2025.

The licensing rule is a product rule and a geographic rule together. A question about which configurations require a license is answered by the 96 GB threshold in this section. A question about how much revenue is exposed is answered by the 14 percent figure, also in this section. The products section names the same threshold only to point here.

Management's mitigation is a second configuration of HIR-3 that stays at or below 96 GB, plus a practice of filing licenses before the requested ship week. Mitigation does not remove the risk. A broader rule that covered the base configuration would put a larger share of revenue into the license process than the 14 percent currently exposed.

## Customer concentration {risk-concentration}

Customer A accounted for 11 percent of fiscal 2024 revenue and 9 percent of fiscal 2023 revenue. The loss of Customer A, or a sharp reduction in its orders, would materially reduce revenue and would leave capacity at Quarry Ridge underused. No other customer accounted for more than 10 percent of revenue in either year.

The relationship is a commercial contract, not an equity investment. Customer A has no board seat. Pricing for Customer A is negotiated annually and was not disclosed. The company does not offer Customer A most-favored pricing on paper, but in practice the negotiated price is the reference the direct sales force uses for other cloud operators of similar scale.

Concentration also shows up in backlog. A meaningful portion of the $2.05 billion backlog at February 2, 2025 is Customer A, but the company does not disclose that portion separately. Readers should not subtract 11 percent of backlog and treat the result as Customer A's orders. Eleven percent is a revenue share, not a backlog share.

## Lattice Silicon supply commitment {risk-supply}

Helios signed a wafer supply agreement with Lattice Silicon in May 2022. The agreement runs through December 2027. It is a take-or-pay arrangement. The remaining minimum purchase commitment was $900 million as of February 2, 2025, compared with $1.15 billion as of January 28, 2024. The decline is purchases made during fiscal 2024, not a renegotiation. The company did not amend the minimums during the year.

If demand for HIR-3 and HIR-4 falls below the volume assumed in the agreement, Helios would still owe the minimum. The obligation is a purchase of wafers, not a debt. It does not sit in the total-debt figure. It is not cancellable for convenience. Lattice Silicon can stop shipments if Helios misses a minimum payment, and a stoppage would halt rack assembly within a quarter because the company does not keep a year of wafer inventory.

The company has a second source for a small power-management device and no second source for the main accelerator wafer. Qualifying a second source would take longer than the remaining term of the agreement. Management therefore treats the Lattice commitment as both a supply plan and a fixed-cost risk. The $900 million remaining is the number that answers a question about the purchase obligation still outstanding at fiscal 2024 year end.

## Quarry Ridge power {risk-power}

The flagship campus at Quarry Ridge, outside San Antonio, has 180 megawatts contracted. The power purchase agreement expires on June 30, 2026. Energy cost at Quarry Ridge was $146 million in fiscal 2024. The company is negotiating a renewal and has not secured replacement power for the period after the agreement expires.

Quarry Ridge is where company-operated clusters run. Customer-owned racks in other halls are not on this contract, and their power cost is the customer's. The $146 million is the company's own energy cost at this one campus. It is not a consolidated utilities expense for every site. Ashburn and Hillsboro are leased halls with power included in rent, and that rent is not in the $146 million.

If the renewal price is materially higher, gross margin on company-operated inference would compress. If the renewal is not signed and replacement power is not found, the company would have to move workloads or idle capacity. Moving the flagship cluster is not practical on a few months' notice, because the power and cooling design is specific to the Quarry Ridge building. The expiration date, June 30, 2026, is the operational deadline management is planning around. No extension has been signed.

## Litigation {risk-litigation}

On March 12, 2024, a putative securities class action, Alvarez v. Helios Systems, was filed in the Western District of Texas. The plaintiffs allege that the company overstated cluster utilization in earnings materials during the second half of fiscal 2023. Helios believes the claims are without merit. No loss contingency was accrued as of February 2, 2025.

The company has not settled the matter and has not offered to settle it. The filing names the company and certain officers. It does not name Lattice Silicon, Customer A, or Northline Scheduler as defendants. An adverse outcome could include damages and a change in how utilization is described. The company cannot estimate a range of loss. Because it cannot estimate a range, and because it believes the claims are without merit, it accrued nothing.

Utilization itself is defined and quantified in the utilization section of this report. This risk factor states the lawsuit. It does not restate the utilization percentages, so that a reader does not treat the litigation summary as the measurement section. Questions about the case name, the court, the allegation, and whether a loss was accrued are answered here. Questions about the 73 percent and 69 percent figures are answered in the utilization section.

## Cluster utilization {utilization}

Average cluster utilization was 73 percent in fiscal 2024 and 69 percent in fiscal 2023. Utilization is the share of accelerator-hours sold versus accelerator-hours available in company-operated clusters. It is not a measure of racks that customers operate themselves, and it is not a measure of a single customer's workload.

Accelerator-hours available exclude hours lost to maintenance windows the company scheduled, and they include hours lost to unplanned outages. A higher number means a larger share of available hours was sold. It does not mean every sold hour was a high-margin hour. Software attached to those hours is reported in software and support revenue, not as a second utilization metric.

The litigation described in the risk factors alleges that materials in the second half of fiscal 2023 overstated this figure. The figures in this section are the figures the company stands by in this Form 10-K: 73 percent for fiscal 2024 and 69 percent for fiscal 2023. The company did not revise the fiscal 2023 figure in this report.

# Item 8. Financial statements, notes

## Northline Scheduler acquisition {acquisition-northline}

On August 15, 2023, Helios acquired Northline Scheduler for $610 million in cash. Goodwill recorded on the acquisition was $430 million. The remainder of the purchase price was allocated to developed technology and to deferred tax effects, which are not itemized in this narrative note. Northline software was folded into the Helios Fabric Compiler during fiscal 2024. The Northline brand is no longer used on a product.

Revenue from Northline-derived software was $188 million in fiscal 2024 and $71 million in the partial fiscal 2023 period after the acquisition closed. The partial-period figure is not annualized in this note. Comparing $188 million with $71 million as if both were full years would overstate the growth rate. The $71 million covers August 15, 2023 through January 28, 2024.

Goodwill of $430 million is tested annually for impairment. The company recorded no impairment in fiscal 2024. The acquisition is the reason research and development headcount and expense stepped up, but the purchase price itself is not part of research and development expense. Cash paid is the reason the fiscal 2024 cash balance is a change from operations and from this investing outflow together. The cash figure in the liquidity section is the ending balance, not the balance before the acquisition. The acquisition closed in fiscal 2023, so the cash outflow was in the prior year. Fiscal 2024 cash is after that outflow.

The note that will appear in the fiscal 2023 Form 10-K states the same purchase price and the same goodwill. This fiscal 2024 note is the one that states the subsequent full-year revenue of $188 million.
